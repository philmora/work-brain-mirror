# Change Agency for AI Adoption

*The organizational change management dimension of getting PMs, engineers, and
domain experts to actually adopt AI tooling — the people problem, not the
technology problem.*

---

## What it is

Change agency for AI adoption is the recognition that the hardest part of
making an organization AI-native isn't the technology — it's getting people
to change how they work. The tools exist. Claude Code works. Cursor works.
The models are capable. But adoption stalls because people are busy, skeptical,
don't see the relevance to their specific workflow, or tried once and got a
bad result and gave up.

Being a change agent means being the person who demonstrates the new workflow,
builds the on-ramps, removes the friction, celebrates early wins, and holds
the space for people to experiment and fail safely. It's less about
evangelism and more about infrastructure: making the AI-assisted path the
path of least resistance.

## Why it matters

The current wave of AI tooling investment at most companies is a bet that
engineers, data scientists, analysts, and PMs will actually adopt the tools
on offer. But budgets and licenses describe outcomes, not the change process.
Someone has to own the "how do we actually get people to adopt this?"
question. That's change agency work, and it's usually invisible until it
fails.

Engineering-side adoption has emerging patterns — champion programs, repo
readiness, weekly learning cadences. PM-side adoption is harder, because
there's no equivalent of "repo readiness" for PM work. It's workflow
readiness, and workflows are personal. That makes change agency even more
important: individual PMs need individual on-ramps.

## Key points

- The champion model works for engineering adoption: one IC per repo, a few
  hours a week, sharing wins and failures in a shared channel. Habits form
  when peers ship.
- PM adoption is harder: no shared artifact to prepare, so the focus shifts
  to safe experimentation environments and exemplars
- The biggest adoption blocker isn't capability — it's the first experience.
  If someone's first AI interaction fails, they may not try again for months.
- Leading by example is non-negotiable. You can't advocate for AI-native
  workflows you don't use yourself. Everything the change agent ships —
  automations, knowledge systems, prototypes — is a demonstration of what's
  possible.
- Honest gap: we don't yet have good metrics for "AI adoption" beyond
  engineering PR merge rates. How do you measure whether a PM's thinking
  improved because they used AI? Open question.
