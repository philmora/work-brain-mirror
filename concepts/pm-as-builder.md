# PM-as-Builder

*The thesis that the PM role can be reinvented by pairing deep technical
product leadership with AI-native building — protecting and expanding builder
time as the north star. The role evolves into "builder" starting now.*

---

## What it is

PM-as-builder is a personal operating thesis. The traditional PM role is
meetings, alignment, stakeholder management, and spec-writing — necessary
work, but work that doesn't directly create artifacts. The thesis: AI-native
tooling has compressed coordination overhead enough that a PM can spend
meaningful time actually building — prototypes, automation, analysis tools,
knowledge systems — while still fulfilling the strategic and stakeholder
responsibilities of the role.

This isn't "PMs should learn to code." It's "the tooling has crossed a
threshold where a technically deep PM can produce real software artifacts,
and that changes the role." The distinction matters because it's not about
replacing engineers — it's about a PM who already thinks in systems and
architectures being able to express that thinking directly in working software.

## Why it matters

Builder time is the scarcest resource. Everything built — tools, automations,
a personal knowledge wiki, prototype workflows — compounds. Every hour in a
meeting produces a conversation that decays. Protecting builder time is a
structural advantage: the PM who builds has tighter feedback loops, better
intuition for engineering tradeoffs, and can validate ideas faster than one
who only writes about them.

This wiki itself is an instance of the thesis. A PM building a Karpathy-style
LLM wiki as a personal knowledge management system — that's not a traditional
PM activity. But it's precisely the kind of thing that compounds over months
and years.

## Key points

- The enablers are specific: a coding-capable LLM with very large context,
  custom tooling extensions, integrations to the day-job systems, scheduled
  automations, and a subagent pattern for parallel work
- Real builder artifacts so far: automated release summaries for multiple eng
  teams, cost-optimization reports, meeting-transcript analysis, daily
  communication digests, weekly OKR readouts — all in production, all running
  without direct supervision
- The risk is real: builder time gets eaten by coordination overhead if not
  actively protected
- The organizational generalization — "if it works for one PM, it should
  scale to others" — is the next test
- Honest check: the thesis requires that the artifacts actually deliver value,
  not just demonstrate capability. The test is whether other people use what
  gets built.
